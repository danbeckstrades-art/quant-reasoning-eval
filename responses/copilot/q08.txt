Which Model Is More Useful?

Model B is far more useful.

Although its accuracy (98.6%) is slightly lower than Model A's (99%), it detects 80 out of 100 fraud cases, while Model A detects none. In fraud detection, missing fraud is usually much more costly than investigating some false alarms.

Why Is Accuracy a Poor Guide Here?

The dataset is highly imbalanced:

Fraud rate = 100/10000=1%100/10000 = 1\%100/10000=1%
Non-fraud rate = 99%99\%99%

A model can achieve 99% accuracy simply by predicting every transaction as non-fraud, as Model A does. The high accuracy comes from correctly classifying the overwhelming majority of legitimate transactions, not from detecting fraud.

Therefore, in imbalanced problems like fraud detection, precision, recall, F1-score, PR curves, and cost-sensitive metrics are much more informative than accuracy alone.

Key insight: Model A has the higher accuracy but is essentially useless for finding fraud. Model B sacrifices only 0.4 percentage points of accuracy yet catches 80% of fraudulent transactions.
