(a) What is wrong with the independent two-sample t-test?

The before and after measurements come from the same 30 users, so the observations are paired, not independent.

An independent two-sample t-test incorrectly treats the before and after samples as unrelated groups and ignores the within-person pairing. This loses information about how each user's score changed and typically gives an incorrect estimate of the standard error.

The correct analysis is a paired t-test, which works with the 30 individual differences:

di=afteri−beforeid_i = \text{after}_i - \text{before}_idi​=afteri​−beforei​

and tests whether the population mean difference is zero.
