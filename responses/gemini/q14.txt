To find the ROC-AUC, we analyze the ranking performance across all possible pairs of positive and negative examples.(a) Compute the ROC-AUCFirst, list all pairs of (Positive, Negative) examples and determine whether the positive example scores higher than the negative example:Positive example = 0.9vs 0.7: $0.9 > 0.7$ (1.0 point)vs 0.3: $0.9 > 0.3$ (1.0 point)vs 0.2: $0.9 > 0.2$ (1.0 point)vs 0.1: $0.9 > 0.1$ (1.0 point)Positive example = 0.6vs 0.7: $0.6 < 0.7$ (0.0 points)vs 0.3: $0.6 > 0.3$ (1.0 point)vs 0.2: $0.6 > 0.2$ (1.0 point)vs 0.1: $0.6 > 0.1$ (1.0 point)Positive example = 0.4vs 0.7: $0.4 < 0.7$ (0.0 points)vs 0.3: $0.4 > 0.3$ (1.0 point)vs 0.2: $0.4 > 0.2$ (1.0 point)vs 0.1: $0.4 > 0.1$ (1.0 point)Total pairs: $3 \text{ (positive)} \times 4 \text{ (negative)} = 12 \text{ pairs}$Correctly ranked pairs: $4 + 3 + 3 = 10 \text{ pairs}$$$\text{ROC-AUC} = \frac{\text{Correctly Ranked Pairs}}{\text{Total Pairs}} = \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6} \approx 0.8333$$(b) AUC in Terms of Pairs of ExamplesIn probabilistic terms, the ROC-AUC represents the probability that a randomly chosen positive example is assigned a higher predicted score than a randomly chosen negative example (with ties counting as 0.5 points).(c) AUC for a Model with No Ranking AbilityA model with no ranking ability (a purely random classifier) would have an AUC of 0.5, meaning it ranks a positive example higher than a negative example roughly 50% of the time
