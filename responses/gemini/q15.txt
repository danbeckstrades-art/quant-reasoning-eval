With 10 weekly checks under the null hypothesis, the probability of encountering at least one false positive grows significantly above the nominal 5% level.(c) Two ways to avoid the problem:Fixed-Horizon Testing (No Peeking): Calculate the required sample size in advance based on the statistical power and minimum detectable effect you want. Run the experiment for the full predetermined duration and calculate the $p$-value once at the end.Sequential Testing (Alpha Spending Methods): Use dynamic p-value thresholds—such as Pocock or O'Brien-Fleming boundaries—or Bayesian A/B testing approaches. These adjust the statistical significance threshold at each interim look so that the total false positive rate across all checks stays capped at 5%.
