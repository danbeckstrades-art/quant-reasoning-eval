This is a classic and insidious form of data leakage known as preprocessing leakage.What is the Problem?When the StandardScaler is fitted on the entire dataset before cross-validation, it calculates the mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$) using every single sample—including the ones that will later end up in the validation folds.During 5-fold cross-validation:The model is trained on 4 folds, but those training features have already been centered and scaled using parameters ($\mu$ and $\sigma$) that incorporate information from the 5th (validation) fold.Information from the validation set has "leaked" into the training process.As a result, the model has an unfair advantage, and the cross-validation score will be overly optimistic, masking how poorly the model will actually perform on truly unseen, out-of-sample data.How Large is the Impact Likely to Be?The severity of the bias depends primarily on the dataset size:Small Datasets (High Impact): On smaller datasets, a few validation samples have a massive influence on the global mean and standard deviation. The leakage can significantly inflate your performance metrics, leading you to believe you have a great model when it is actually overfitted.Large Datasets (Lower Impact, but Still Flawed): On massive datasets, the global mean and standard deviation closely approximate the mean and standard deviation of any individual random fold. The numerical difference might be small, but it is conceptually and methodologically incorrect. In production or competitions, this can still degrade performance or invalidate your scientific rigor.
