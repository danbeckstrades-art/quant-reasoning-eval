To cut the margin of error in half (from $\pm 4.4\%$ down to $\pm 2.2\%$), you must sample 2,000 people.Step-by-Step ExplanationThe margin of error ($ME$) for a population proportion depends inversely on the square root of the sample size ($\sqrt{n}$).$$ME = z^* \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}}$$Where:$z^*$ is the critical value for the confidence level ($z^* \approx 1.96$ for 95% confidence)$\hat{p}$ is the sample proportion ($\hat{p} = 0.50$)$n$ is the sample sizeBecause the sample size $n$ sits under a square root in the denominator:$$\text{Margin of Error } (ME) \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$$To halve the margin of error ($\frac{1}{2} \cdot ME$), you must scale $n$ by a factor $k$:$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{k \cdot n}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \implies \sqrt{k} = 2 \implies k = 2^2 = 4$$Thus, to cut the margin of error in half, the sample size must be quadrupled.$$\text{New Sample Size} = 4 \times 500 = 2,000$$Verification with the FormulaLet's calculate the exact margins of error for both sample sizes under the given parameters ($z^* = 1.96$, $p = 0.50$).1. Initial Sample Size ($n = 500$)$$ME_1 = 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{0.50 \cdot (1 - 0.50)}{500}}$$$$ME_1 = 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{0.25}{500}} = 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{0.0005} \approx 1.96 \cdot 0.02236 = 0.0438 \approx \mathbf{4.4\%}$$2. Quadrupled Sample Size ($n = 2,000$)$$ME_2 = 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{0.50 \cdot (1 - 0.50)}{2000}}$$$$ME_2 = 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{0.25}{2000}} = 1.96 \cdot \sqrt{0.000125} \approx 1.96 \cdot 0.01118 = 0.0219 \approx \mathbf{2.2\%}$$Ratio Comparison$$\frac{ME_2}{ME_1} = \frac{0.0219}{0.0438} = \frac{1}{2}$$This confirms that polling 2,000 people produces exactly half the margin of error of polling 500 people.
