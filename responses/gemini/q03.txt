This apparent paradox occurs because of a confounding variable: baseline illness severity combined with uneven allocation.Mild cases have a much higher baseline recovery rate ($\sim 80\text{--}90\%$) than severe cases ($\sim 30\text{--}35\%$).The new treatment group was heavily skewed toward mild patients ($80\%$ mild vs. $20\%$ severe), pulling its aggregate recovery rate up significantly.The standard care group was heavily skewed toward severe patients ($80\%$ severe vs. $20\%$ mild), dragging its aggregate recovery rate down.Which treatment the data favors:
The data favors Standard Care. When controlling for patient severity, standard care produces a higher recovery rate for both mild patients ($+10\%$) and severe patients ($+5\%$). The overall higher recovery rate for the new treatment is an illusion caused by assigning easier-to-treat patients to the new treatment group.
