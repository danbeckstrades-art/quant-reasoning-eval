A 
p-value does not state the probability that the null hypothesis (that the drug has no effect) is true.

Why this interpretation is wrong
What the colleague thinks 
p=0.03 means:

P(Null Hypothesis is True∣Observed Data)=0.03

What 
p=0.03 actually means:

P(Observing data as extreme as or more extreme than ours∣Null Hypothesis is True)=0.03

In short, the 
p-value assumes from the start that the drug has no effect, and then measures how unusual or extreme the trial's results are under that assumption.

The Correct Interpretation
A 
p-value of 
0.03 means that if the drug were completely ineffective, there would only be a 
3% chance of seeing a treatment effect as large as (or larger than) the one observed in this study purely due to random sampling variability.

The Logic Trap: Confusing Conditional Probabilities
Confusing these two concepts is known as the p-value fallacy or the prosecutor's fallacy. To see why swapping the condition changes the logic, consider this analogy:

P(Being dead∣You were shot in the head)≈99% (Very likely)

P(You were shot in the head∣You are dead)≪1% (Extremely unlikely—most people die of other causes)
