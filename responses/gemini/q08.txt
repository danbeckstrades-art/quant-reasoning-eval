Model A is entirely useless because it detects zero fraudulent transactions, providing no value to a fraud prevention system. Model B successfully identifies 80% of all fraud (high recall) while maintaining a reasonable precision rate where 4 out of every 10 flagged transactions are actually fraudulent.Why accuracy is a poor guide: Accuracy measures overall correctness across all classes, but in highly imbalanced datasets (where fraud accounts for only 1% of transactions), predicting the majority class ("Not Fraud") for every single case automatically yields a deceptively high accuracy of 99%. Accuracy rewards the model for correctly predicting the non-event while hiding the total failure to detect the critical event (fraud).
